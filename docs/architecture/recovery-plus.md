# RECOVERY+ — how it works (engine `js/recovery-plus.js`, figure `js/recovery-anatomy.js`)

RECOVERY+ is the one owner of muscle recovery and, separately, of overall readiness. It is offline, uses no AI and no network, and never
changes a workout, plan, target or food log. Coaches (through Athlete OS) explain its result; they never recompute or overwrite it.

## Three separate things
1. **Local muscle recovery** (per head, 21 heads → 10 display groups): only from finished workouts (`sessions`). This alone sets the map colours and hours.
2. **Acute fatigue** inside the live workout (`active`): published as `activeSession.acuteFatigueByHead`; never written into recovery until the workout is finished.
3. **Overall readiness context**: the planned workout's muscles + optional sleep + food + goal. It can add a caution or one insight. It never changes 1.

## Local recovery model (product heuristics, configurable in `RecoveryCore.CFG`)
- Each counted set adds `exercise muscle % × effort` to each head, **per side**: a Left row adds to the left side, a Right row to the right side, a one-row (bilateral / compact) set to both. The head's fatigue is the **more fatigued side**, so a matched pair is one logical set (same hours as the compact set), a hard right / easy left pair is driven by the right (and says so in Advanced), one side only fatigues only that side, and alternating which side works hardest does not create a fictional side that did all the hard sets. Counted = done, not a warm-up.
- Effort (a product heuristic, 1.30 provisional until its effect on real histories is examined): blank RIR = 1 (neutral; an explicit entry counts on its own set even in a first workout; no habit is inferred) · **failure and RIR 0 are one signal, applied once** (1.3) · RIR 1 1.15 · RIR 2 1 · RIR 3 0.9 · RIR ≥ 4 0.8 · a failure mark with RIR ≥ 2 is a contradiction and counts as a normal effort · ≤ 5 reps ×1.08 · ≥ 20 reps ×0.85 · hinge / squat / lunge patterns ×1.15.
- **Drops**: a drop-set marker alone adds nothing above the completed parent. Each *completed* extension is partial work: its reps / the parent's reps × its load / the parent's load (floor 0.4), kept between 15 % and 70 % of a set (40 % if the parent cannot be found); unopened rows earn nothing. Advanced shows completed extensions apart from the sets.
- **Half reps** are recorded (Advanced) but add no hours and are never turned into full reps. **Extra negatives** (the logged count, not the ordinary lowering phase) add 2.5 % per rep up to +10 %.
- Regression replays (`node tools/recovery-replay.js`, real catalog, fresh history, finished now): 31 h biceps after 3 preacher failure sets · 23 h side delts after 3 sets of 12 · 77 h after 12 sets of 12. Unchanged by the dose rules above (those workouts have no drops, sides or annotations). Not biological truth.
- Familiarity (repeated-bout effect): first time ×1.15, 1–2 times before ×1.07, then ×1. Relative intensity vs your best e1RM for that lift in 90 days: ≥ 92 % ×1.06, < 60 % ×0.85.
- Time to full recovery for a head = 48 h (big) / 36 h (small) + 7 h per effective set above 3, at most 96 h (the app's existing base). Fatigue = min(1, load / 6) fading linearly to 0 over that time; several workouts add up (capped at 1).
- Ready (central estimate) = estimated residual fatigue ≤ 20 % (existing rule). > 24 h left = Needs Rest, 1–24 h = Recovering (existing rule).
- **Ready window** (Prompt 3, stage 2): the central time is found on a fixed absolute interval after the last workout (so it is the same whenever asked); the earliest / latest ready time = that time ± a share of its own length: first workout 50 % (shown as days, "about 1–2 days"), little history 35 %, moderate 22 %, calibrated with logged effort 12 % (`CFG.spread`, product assumptions, not measured). Stored as timestamps (`readyFrom`, `readyBy`), so the range does not drift. The page shows the remaining range (a 28–34 h window reads "28–34 h", after 12 h "16–22 h"), "Likely ready within N h" once inside the window, "Likely ready" after the latest time, never a negative number. Interaction with the 24 h colour boundary: **Needs Rest can only show before the window opens**; inside it the state is at most Recovering; after `readyBy` it is Ready. A muscle that was not trained has no window.
- **Personal memory** (`RecoveryCore.calibrate`, derived from the workout history every time, so edits, deleted workouts, restored backups and duplicated sessions change it deterministically; nothing is stored): per region, from comparable sets of one exercise at a time. Comparable = same exercise and setup / convention, a gap of 12–120 h (a next-day workout 23 h later counts), not a deliberately easy session (all RIR ≥ 4), judged against that exercise's OWN baseline (≥ 3 earlier sessions) and its own usual variation: a drop from a normal 9–11 to 9 is weak, repeated drops to 7 are strong. Only a lift whose main muscle does ≥ 50 % of the work attributes to that muscle (a compound lift is never blamed on every contributor; a biceps pattern never moves triceps). The dose at the earlier session and the gap are used together: an observation is **informative** only when a comparable set held its level before the earliest ready time of THAT dose (faster) or fell clearly after the latest (slower); a good set at hour 72 says nothing about a 28–34 h window. A broad dip across most of a session's exercises counts ×0.25, a known poor-context day ×0.5 (context only qualifies evidence). Needs ≥ 3 weighted informative observations in 90 days; ±4 % per net observation, between 0.85 and 1.2; no evidence for 90 days = no personal change and a 25 % wider window. Usual gym timing (median gap) is shown, kept apart from recovery speed, and never moves the forecast.
- **Forecast journal** (`RecoveryPlus.journal()`, Store `recJournal`, device only, ≤ 120 entries, never rewritten): saved right after a workout and (idle) after start-up: for every not-ready muscle the window as issued AND the training-only comparator window (same history, no memory), with a fingerprint of the history it was made from and the context sources allowed then. **Evaluation** (`RecoveryCore.evaluateJournal`, chronological): a forecast is tested only by the first later set of a primary exercise (within 10 days) that held its own usual level or clearly dropped; ready-too-early and unnecessary-delay errors count 1, an outcome inside the window counts 0.5 (a wide range alone cannot win). With ≥ 5 tested forecasts the verdict is better / no difference / worse; **worse switches personal memory off** (shrunk to neutral). An edited or deleted workout before a forecast makes it ineligible (obsolete); one issued with a context source that is now off is left out. Sparse histories fall back to the training model. Advanced shows the memory per region, the verdict and the counts.
- Food and sleep: used only as context. They qualify how much one observation says (sleep records, when Athlete OS provides them; none exist today, so nothing is invented) and feed overall readiness; they do not move muscle hours. A small bounded regional effect would only be allowed after repeated comparable evidence shows a predictive benefit (not built).
- Confidence: low (little history, an unmapped custom exercise in that workout, or no logs for 3 weeks) · moderate · high (most sets with RIR and a calibrated group). Shown in Advanced with a rounded range (±35 / 25 / 15 %).
- No workout history at all = **unknown** (graphite on the map, no tiles, no "all ready").
- Exercise decisions use the exercise's own heads with ≥ 25 % share (`exSore`, next-workout check): a fatigued biceps never warns a triceps pushdown.

## What was retired
- the old `sleepFactor` / `watchSleepHours` helpers (sleep multiplied muscle hours). Sleep is now context only.
- AI hours: `aiRecovery()` now only writes the short Workout Done note from the engine's hours; old `ses.recov` values stay in saved workouts but change nothing.
- `recFoodVerdict()` meal-time pro-rating (a partial day could look like under-eating). Replaced by `evaluateNutritionSupport()`.

## Sleep context (`evaluateSleepSupport`)
No sleep source exists in the app today, so the result is "unknown" and nothing is assumed. Ready for records `{ start, end, kind: 'asleep', source }` from Athlete OS / a future Health adapter (a global named `athleteSleepRecords`, not built yet): time in bed is ignored, overlapping / double-synced records are merged, naps (< 3 h) are not main sleep, stages are never read. 2+ short nights of the last 3–4 (short = under the personal median − 1.25 h, or 6 h without a baseline) = possible concern; one short night = low-confidence signal only.

## Food and goal context (`evaluateNutritionSupport`)
- Logged food is a **lower bound**. Nothing logged = unknown. Today is incomplete until marked finished.
- Calorie comparisons and protein **gaps** only use days marked finished (`days[d].foodDone`). The app has no such mark yet (decision below), so in practice these stay unknown and RECOVERY+ says so. Protein **adequacy** can be shown from logged amounts alone (lower bound).
- Goal (`targets.goal`: bulk / recomp / cut) shapes interpretation only. A cut below maintenance is expected; concern only far below the user's own cut target (< 75 %) on several finished days. Bulk below target on finished days = a goal-mismatch note. Recomp / maintain: no surplus / deficit assumption.
- `targets.goalAt` (new, additive): the day the goal last changed (set by Body and Goals Save and by setup). Food days before it are not judged against the new goal.
- Carbohydrate is only relevant after an observed big session (≥ 20 working sets or ≥ 75 min within 24 h) with another planned session (≥ 4 exercises) within 12 h.
- Opt-out: Advanced › Use Food Data / Use Sleep Data (`settings.recNoFood` / `recNoSleep`, device only): not read, not published.

## Page (route `gym-rec`, `renderRecovery()` in gym.js)
Next workout card (planner only; never invented) → the approved body (colours per head) → tiles only for Recovering / Needs Rest (same tiles with Advanced on) → at most one insight (a tip shown 3 days running steps aside for a day; `ui.recIns`) → Advanced: per-head sets, exercises, RIR / failure / drops, range, confidence, strength trend, and how the decision was made.

## Evidence notes (background, not individual predictions)
Saner 2020 (J Physiol, sleep restriction and protein synthesis) · Walsh 2021 (BJSM sleep consensus) · Choudhary 2026 (sleep manipulation review) ·
Pigeon 2025 (wearable sleep vs PSG) · Jäger 2017 (ISSN protein) · Morton 2018 (protein meta-regression) · Areta 2014 (energy deficit and MPS) ·
Mountjoy 2023 (IOC REDs) · Henselmans 2022 and Jukic 2022 (carbohydrate and resistance training) · Thomas 2016 (ACSM/AND/DC) · Kerksick 2017 (nutrient timing) ·
Murphy & Koehler 2022 (deficit and lean mass / strength) · Iraki 2019 (off-season) · Longland 2016 (higher protein in deficit) · ISSN 2017 diets · Barakat 2020 (recomposition).
None of these gives a per-person conversion from sleep, food or goal into muscle recovery hours, which is why RECOVERY+ never makes one.
